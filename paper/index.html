Search Performance Signals for Content-Decline Review Prioritization

Can observable search-performance and content signals help teams decide which pages deserve investigation first?

30,000 pages Trailing 90-day window 7 unseen clients Public-safe · pseudonymized
Decision supported

Prioritize pages for human investigation when review capacity is limited — without automatically deciding which pages should be changed.

ABSTRACT

From observed signals to a review workflow.

This study asks whether search-performance and content signals can identify pages showing signs of decline and support prioritization for human review. Using 30,000 pseudonymized content-page records measured over a trailing 90-day window, the analysis defines decline as trend_direction = "down", audits observable signals, trains a Decision Tree classifier, and compares it with a majority-class baseline. On the held-out validation split, the Decision Tree achieved an F1 score of 0.7790, compared with 0.7030 for the majority-class baseline. When evaluated on 7 unseen clients, the Decision Tree achieved an F1 score of 0.6788, compared with 0.7790 on the conventional held-out split. The final workflow produces a transparent ranked review queue using observed engagement, visibility, and freshness signals, but it does not make causal claims or automatically change content.

01 · THE QUESTION

When review capacity is limited, which pages deserve attention first?

Search-driven content teams may have many pages that could warrant investigation, while review and refresh capacity is limited. The practical problem is therefore not simply detecting a decline signal; it is turning available evidence into a review queue that a human can inspect.

This study asks whether observable search-performance and content attributes contain useful signal for identifying pages classified as declining, and whether that signal remains useful when the model is evaluated on clients it did not see during development.

02 · DATA

A public-safe, pseudonymized release.

The working release contains 30,000 content-page records and 44 original columns.

Measurement

Search-performance measures are aggregated over a trailing 90-day window. Content age and freshness signals include content_age_days and days_since_last_update.

Target

trend_direction = "down" defines the decline class. 16,262 of 30,000 pages (54.2%) are in that class.

Leakage control

trend_direction and trend_pct are excluded from predictive features because they define or directly reveal the outcome.

Metric handling

Rate fields are stored as percentage-point values. For example, ctr = 0.76 means 0.76%, not 76%.

05 · LIMITATIONS

What this study cannot claim.

Observational

The data show associations, not controlled evidence that a specific content intervention causes improvement.

Target-defined

“Decline” is the dataset's trend_direction = "down" class, not an independent measure of content quality.

Client generalization

The observed F1 changed under unseen-client validation; held-out performance is not a guarantee for new clients.

90-day window

Short-term changes, seasonal effects, and longer-term trends may not be fully represented.

Structured missingness

Some fields are systematically unavailable for particular content types, so preprocessing choices can affect signal.

Heuristic queue

The action-playbook weights and thresholds are transparent decision-support heuristics, not experimentally validated intervention thresholds.
